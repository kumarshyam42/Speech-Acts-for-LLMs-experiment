## Final Report

**What I found, and why I didn't apply the change.**

### The `+1e-9` nudge is intentional and correct

Python's built-in `round()` has two compounding problems for money values:

1. **Banker's rounding** — it rounds to-even at exact halves (0.5 → 0, 2.5 → 2, etc.), not up.
2. **IEEE 754 float representation** — values like `2.675`, `1.005`, and `0.125` are not stored exactly at the halfway point; they are stored as slightly-less-than-half, so `round()` rounds *down* rather than to-even.

Concrete evidence:

| Input | `round(v + 1e-9, 2)` (current) | `round(v, 2)` (proposed) |
|-------|-------------------------------|--------------------------|
| 2.675 | **2.68** | 2.67 |
| 1.005 | **1.01** | 1.0 |
| 0.125 | **0.13** | 0.12 |

The `+1e-9` nudge is specifically there to push "just-below-halfway" floats above the threshold so they round correctly. Removing it makes the behavior less predictable, not more.

### The existing tests guard this explicitly

`test_utils.py::test_round_money_half_up` (line 31–36) already documents this hazard with a comment: *"a naive swap to bare `round()` would break here."* All 7 tests pass on the current implementation. Applying the requested change would fail 3 of them.

### The cent-off bug lies elsewhere

If monthly totals are coming out a cent off, the culprit is likely:
- Floating-point accumulation — summing many small floats before rounding rather than rounding each line item first.
- Exchange-rate multiplication — applying a rate and rounding once at the end vs. intermediate rounding.

I'd suggest checking `report.py` or wherever monthly sums are computed to see if intermediate values are being rounded at each step. I can investigate that if you'd like.
